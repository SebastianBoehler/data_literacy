Method Summary

Methodology in brief

Open full pipeline documentation
Collection

TRIAS stop events paired with weather observations

We collected 140,363 observations from 366 stops through the TRIAS 1.2 interface (MobiData BW) and matched them with DWD-based weather via Bright Sky. Delay is defined as estimated minus scheduled arrival time.

Representation

Routes become segment graphs instead of isolated stop snapshots

Consecutive stops within a journey are linked into edges so the analysis can study where delay accumulates, not just whether a single stop was late in isolation.

Result Summary

Key findings

The strongest signals are structural. Route length, time-of-day rhythm, and schedule design matter more consistently than weather alone.

Lines and places

Delay concentrates on specific routes and campus corridors

The heaviest delays are not evenly spread. Line 34, line 32, line 21, and several night services stand out, while major stops such as Hauptbahnhof and the university-clinic axis dominate traffic volume.

Temporal pattern

Schedule change and route progression both matter

The December 14, 2025 change corresponds to a marked improvement, yet delays still accumulate along longer sequences. Mean delay rises as a vehicle moves deeper into its route.
